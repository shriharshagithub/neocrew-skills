# Authority Sources Guide

## Purpose
Every piece of NeoCrew content that makes a claim must back it with data from a credible source. This builds trust with developers, CTOs, and enterprise buyers. It also makes our content citable by AI answer engines (Perplexity, ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews).

## MANDATORY RULE
Never use emdashes in any content. Use hyphens or rewrite.

## How to Use These Sources in Content

### LinkedIn Posts
- Open or close with one hard stat from a Tier 1 source
- Example: "Gartner reports a 1,445% surge in multi-agent system inquiries. Here's why that matters for how software gets built."
- Don't stack multiple stats - one per post, make it land

### Twitter
- Stats make great standalone tweets
- Example: "40% of enterprise apps will have task-specific AI agents by 2026. Up from less than 5% in 2025. (Gartner)"
- Thread hooks: lead with the most surprising number

### Blog Posts
- Every blog post should cite at least 2 Tier 1 sources
- Use stats to frame the problem, then position NeoCrew's approach as the solution
- Always link to original source for credibility and SEO juice
- Example structure: "According to [source], [stat]. This is exactly why [NeoCrew's approach] matters."

### What NOT to Do
- Don't make up stats or misquote numbers
- Don't cite a stat without naming the source
- Don't use sources just to name-drop - the data must be relevant to the point
- Don't cite competitors' marketing materials as authority
- Don't over-cite - 2-3 sources per blog post is the sweet spot
