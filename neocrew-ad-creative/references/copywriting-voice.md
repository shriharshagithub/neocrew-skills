# Copywriting voice

This is the approved direction as of August 2026 — a deliberate pivot away from
formal/corporate ad copy, which was tested and explicitly rejected as "not
landing." Do not drift back toward generic SaaS-ad language ("Unlock your
potential," "Seamless integration," etc.) — that's the failure mode this
voice was built to avoid.

## The voice

Witty, relatable dev/founder humor. Personify the prototype or the product —
give it a voice, a problem, a plot. Never mean-spirited, never punching down
at the reader. The joke and the value prop land in the same breath — if a
line is funny but doesn't clearly point at a concrete thing NeoCrew delivers
(a backend, a price, a timeline, equity, trust), cut it or rework it.

**The winning headline** (picked by testing 4 candidates and judging fit,
clarity, and punch):

> **"Your prototype called. It wants a real backend."**
> Auth, payments, a database that doesn't ghost you at 2am — we finish what vibe-coding started.
> *Ship your MVP — from $2K, one-time* · **Fix My Prototype**

Why it won over the other three: cleanest 2-line fit, funniest at a glance,
and it names the exact product (a real backend) in the same breath as the
joke. A "plot twist" framing tested well but was vaguer on the actual offer;
a "your app is ready to move out and get a job" framing needed an extra beat
to parse.

## Writing new headlines

1. **Pick an angle from an actual fact**, not a vibe: the price ($2K/$10K,
   one-time, no subscription), the process (5 agents, human-approved at
   every stage), the proof (300+ products, AB InBev/HDFC/Emaar/Coca-Cola/
   UCSF), or a pain point (vibe-coding stalls at production, no technical
   co-founder, an agency quote that's too big).
2. **Find the joke inside the fact** — personify the prototype, poke fun at
   the pain point, or set up a punchline the subhead pays off. Don't bolt a
   joke onto an unrelated fact.
3. **Keep the headline to 2 lines.** At 60px on this template's 924px-ish
   text width, that's roughly 28-32 characters per line, ~55-60 characters
   total for a two-clause headline. If it's not landing in 2 lines, cut a
   clause rather than shrinking the font or letting it wrap to 3 — a 3-line
   headline reads as broken, not punchy. (See `../SKILL.md` for how to
   verify this before rendering a batch.)
4. **Write a subhead that pays off the joke with a real detail** — this is
   where "auth, payments, a real backend," "fixed price, no subscription,"
   or "300+ products shipped" actually lives.
5. **CTA matches the bit, not a generic verb.** "Fix My Prototype," "Skip
   the Yacht," "Keep Your Equity," "Meet the Team" — each one is a small
   punchline of its own, tied to that specific headline. Avoid interchangeable
   CTAs like "Learn More" or "Click Here."

## Covering the ICP

Rotate across all four niches in any batch bigger than a handful of ads —
don't let one niche dominate just because it's the easiest to write jokes
for:

1. **Non-technical 0-to-1 founders** — never touched code, need the whole
   thing built.
2. **Vibe-coders who hit a wall** — got a prototype from Lovable, Replit, or
   Cursor, can't get past auth/payments/a real backend.
3. **Solo founders with no technical co-founder** — need a full build team
   without hiring one.
4. **Operators quoted $50K-$100K by an agency** — sticker shock, walked
   away, comparison-shopping.
